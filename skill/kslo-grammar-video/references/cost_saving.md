# Cut cost without lowering quality

The first video paid for a reusable series library. For a normal future topic, preserve that investment and spend credits only where a new approved topic truly needs them. **Never save credits by skipping an approval, pronunciation, readability or final QA gate.**

| Do this | What it saves | Keep this quality gate |
|---|---|---|
| **Reuse the three approved library clips**: `intro_v2`, `transition_v2`, `outro_v1`, their aligned refs and the generic lines exactly as supplied. | **Zero video generation** for a normal new topic. Seedance was the largest cost: the first video generated six clips (two variants each of intro, transition and outro). | Verify clips remain keyed/stable, natural-speed, lip-aligned and gesture-correct in the encoded video. Regenerate only if Mr Kum asks or a clip fails QA; update the public library if replaced. |
| **Use Kayla sprites/face patches, not generated talking video, in body scenes.** Use the PNGtuber mouth flap/blinks/reactions in the local engine. | Avoids per-scene/per-line video credits entirely. | Check avatar cut-out, expression cue, safe layout and text clearance in full-resolution frames. |
| **Reuse the approved character and icon library.** The first project spent 3 image calls on design options, 1 on the green keyframe/avatar base, 2 on expression variants and 1 icon-sheet call for 20 icons. For a new topic, generate **at most one new icon sheet**, and only when a required icon cannot be reused. | No recurring character/image generation; one sheet is cheaper and stylistically safer than one icon per call. | Slice, inspect and approve every new icon; check physical meaning/legibility, glyph/layout clearance and visual consistency with the Neon Stream system. |
| **Reuse `theme.wav`, `study_bed.wav` and supplied SFX forever.** | The first two music calls (theme + study bed) become zero-cost for future videos. | Keep music ducked under speech, preserve −16 LUFS mix/peak target and verify black/silent ending. |
| **Start with the design-led HTML system on day one.** Build lesson scenes in `proto/scenes/` and local Chromium, not the old PIL/PowerPoint-style body renderer. | Avoids repeating the first project's expensive whole-video conversion after it was already built. Local HTML/SVG/Python rendering uses compute time and conversation turns, **not generation credits**. | Read `proto/DESIGN.md`, full-resolution still-review every scene and preserve all design rules/holds. Do not use old PIL merely because it is quicker to draft. |
| **Approve the body script before audio or visuals.** Show the proposal/plan, word-for-word scene table, changes from the book and quiz key first. | Prevents discarded renders, retakes and scene rewrites. | Do not substitute script approval with a verbal summary. Keep an auditable source of truth. |
| **Batch visual review before a full render.** Produce a complete key-stills gallery (entrance, busiest state, review hold for every scene) and obtain one explicit batched teacher approval. | The first video needed 11 feedback rounds. One gallery catches layout/reading defects before expensive full rendering and repeated conversation turns. | Inspect full-resolution stills, not only contact sheets; use exact PNG hashes/current assets; block stale replacement assets. |
| **Batch feedback and change only affected SIDs.** Record decisions in `NOTES.md`, collect a coherent fix list, then use `python3 build.py render <SID ...>` followed by `audio` and `mux`. | Avoids repeated all-video rerenders and context loss. | After a timing/audio change, rebuild final audio, captions, transcript checks and MP4. Run a full final render/QA before delivery. |
| **Keep `NOTES.md` current after every decision, defect and chosen take.** Record the cause, repair, measurement and prevention rule. | Avoids rediscovery and contradictory rework after context compaction or agent hand-off. | Include dates/times, selected take IDs and what final QA proved; do not silently overwrite history. |
| **Use parallel scene builders only for 5+ comparable scenes.** Give each builder `proto/DESIGN.md`, fixed SID ownership and required still evidence. | Reduces elapsed work/conversation cost on a large conversion without duplicating setup for a small change. | Integrate each scene through a common full-resolution consistency pass; run all builder scenes before final audio/mux. Never let builders edit shared engine files. |
| **Record one clean TTS take per line after applying known respellings.** The first video had about 27 body lines plus library lines; retakes included intro ×3, outro ×5, one quiz line ×3, a sentence end ×3 and 'Is that allowed?' ×7. Apply `K S Low`, `Ching Yee`, `red`, `liv`, `ing`, `ed`, dates and risk treatments before take 1. | Cuts repeat TTS calls dramatically. Library lines are never re-recorded for a normal topic. | Listen to actual pronunciation/stress/final consonants. `en-GB` and ASR spelling are not sufficient proof. |
| **Repair one faulty word/short phrase instead of re-recording a whole approved line.** Splice at a quiet boundary with short fades; preserve guards and loudness. | Reuses an otherwise excellent take and avoids several full-line TTS calls. | Audition the join, run `prep_voice.py`/`word_times.py`, and test final mixed consonant audibility where relevant. Do not splice blindly at copied timestamps. |
| **Use free local `faster-whisper small.en` for word timing and transcript verification.** Reserve `manus-analyze-video` listening checks for truly risky words: homographs, named places, UK/US vowel concern, lexical stress or final stop. | Avoids analysis-credit spend on routine lines and broad unreliable 'quality' prompts. | Still run two independent final transcriptions and use blind IPA listening for genuinely disputed pronunciation. Never let ASR certify an accent. |
| **Reuse local graphics and icon primitives for diagrams.** Use CSS/SVG timelines, formula tokens, maps, arrows/ticks/crosses and the supplied hand. Generate no topic art unless a real visual explanation needs it. | Local deterministic graphics require no generation credits and are quick to correct precisely. | Follow semantic colour code, glyph check, anti-aliasing, cue timing and full-resolution readability checks. |
| **Render only after still QA, then render only the changed scene during iteration.** `build.py render <SID>` uses the local six-worker pool; a reference full design-led video took about 10 minutes. | Saves compute time and avoids generating/inspecting unrelated segments. | Re-render the complete programme for final delivery; inspect encoded joins and video frames, not only a segment. |
| **Keep clean library audio and retime only video.** | Avoids voice re-recording and prevents audio mangling caused by stretching a voice to fit lips. | Inspect retime speed/contact sheets/duplicate frames; reject slow-motion tails and gesture mismatch. |

## A lean, safe future-topic sequence

1. Reuse assets with `fetch_assets.sh` and initialise a project. Smoke-test the reference build.
2. Get title/items, source-script and design-map approval **before** TTS or scene work.
3. Apply known respellings; make one line-per-call body take; inspect only the risky words intensely.
4. Build all topic graphics locally in the established design-led templates. Reuse sprites/icons/music/SFX/library clips.
5. Generate one comprehensive stills gallery; receive batched feedback; record it in `NOTES.md`.
6. Re-render only changed SIDs while iterating. Then render/audio/mux once for the full current version.
7. Keep the full QA gates: pronunciation checks, layout/readability, two-source transcript verification, caption integrity, loudness/peak, encoded-frame checks, clip smoothness and black/silent ending.

> The economy is deliberate reuse and earlier decisions, **not** a reduction in review quality. The costliest hidden item in the first video was the 11-round iteration cycle, especially the late conversion from the old PIL look to the approved HTML design system.
