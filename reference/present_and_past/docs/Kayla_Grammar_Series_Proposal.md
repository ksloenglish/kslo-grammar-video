# Kayla's Grammar Lab: series proposal and scripts for approval

*K S Lo English · draft v1 · 19 October 2026 · prepared for Mr Kum*

This document sets out the design concept, recommended length, production approach and every script that needs your approval before anything is built. Nothing below has been generated as video yet. The only media made so far are the three Kayla design sheets and three voice samples, so that you can choose.

## 1. Series concept: how it differs from the vocab series

The vocab series is a calm, navy-and-gold quiz show hosted by a tutor, Miss K. The grammar series should feel like dropping into a friendly VTuber's study stream. Kayla is a study buddy rather than a teacher, so she speaks like a slightly older schoolmate: upbeat, encouraging and occasionally dramatic ("Plot twist!").

| Element | Vocab series (Miss K) | Proposed grammar series (Kayla) |
|---|---|---|
| Feel | Polished quiz show | Upbeat VTuber study stream, the "Grammar Lab" |
| Palette | Navy, gold, coral | Midnight violet background with neon cyan, hot pink and sunshine yellow |
| Type | Bebas Neue and Poppins | A rounded, bubbly display font for titles and a clean sans for sentences (confirmed in mockups) |
| Host on screen | Badge with voice rings | Kayla's animated "facecam" in the bottom-right corner: she talks, blinks, bobs and reacts throughout the explanation |
| Motion style | Smooth slides, film-leader countdown | Snappy pop-and-bounce stickers, word blocks that snap together, animated timelines and glitch-wipe transitions |
| Branding | Logo at start and end | K S Lo English logo at the **start** (logo sting), in the **body** (small header logo on every screen) and at the **end** (end card). The logo is always drawn as-is, with no glow, plate or box |

**Fixed colour code for the whole series.** Students will learn to read colours as grammar: **yellow = simple tenses**, **pink = continuous tenses**, **cyan = time and signal words** (every day, now, yesterday, when, while), **green tick = correct** and **red cross = wrong**. Every example sentence highlights its verb in its tense colour. You can reuse the same code in worksheets and slides.

## 2. Recommended length

I recommend **3 to 4 minutes per video, never more than 5**. That means one grammar idea per video: about 20 seconds of opening, 2 to 3 minutes of explanation, a 30-second quiz, a 10-second cheat sheet and a 15-second outro. Each new idea on screen lasts no more than 10 to 20 seconds.

The three textbook pages for Unit 8 contain four separate ideas, which would make one video of roughly 10 minutes. I therefore suggest **four short episodes**. The timings below are estimates from the scripts in Section 6 and will be confirmed against the recorded voice.

| Episode | Topic | Textbook source | Body | Total with intro and outro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ep 1 | Simple Present vs Present Continuous | Page 52 (1.1, 1.2) | about 2:47 | **about 3:24** |
| Ep 2 | Simple Past vs Past Continuous | Page 53 (1.3, 1.4) | about 2:15 | **about 2:52** |

If you would rather have a single Unit 8 video, the same four parts can be joined with one intro and one outro, giving about 9 minutes. I would not recommend that for S2 attention spans.

## 3. Production approach and cost

Your concept is correct: **motion graphics should carry almost everything**. They are rendered from code here, so they cost almost nothing per video, every word is spelt exactly as written, and a correction means re-rendering one scene rather than regenerating footage. I would refine the plan in one important way.

**Kayla in the body should be an animated still-image avatar, not generated video.** Many real VTubers stream with a simple avatar whose mouth moves with their voice. I would create a set of about 8 still images of Kayla once (mouth closed, half-open and open; eyes blinking; happy wink; surprised; thinking). The renderer then animates them in time with her recorded voice, adds blinking and a gentle bob, and switches expressions on cue: a wink for correct answers, surprise for "Plot twist!" and thinking during quiz countdowns. She is therefore present and talking for the whole video at no extra cost per episode. Lip-synced video for 3 minutes of narration would be the most expensive item in the project, and it is unnecessary.

| Item | Made | Method | Cost profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kayla master design, keyframe on green screen and about 8 avatar stills | Once | Image generation and editing from your chosen design | Small, one-off |
| Intro, transition and outro clips (Section 5) | Once, reused in every video | Video generation with lip-sync, two variants each, keeping the better one (as for Miss K) | The main one-off cost, about 30 seconds of footage in total |
| Logo sting, end card, layouts, timelines, word blocks, quizzes and cheat sheets | Reusable templates; content changes per video | Code-rendered motion graphics | Almost free |
| Kayla's narration | Per video | Text-to-speech in the chosen voice, checked line by line | Small |
| Pictures | Per video, only where they genuinely help | Mostly icons drawn in code; at most a few generated sticker illustrations (for example, the shark scene) | Small or none |
| Music | Once | A new signature background track so the series does not sound like the vocab videos; existing sound effects reused | Small, one-off |

After the one-off library is made, **a normal episode needs no video generation at all**, exactly like the vocab series format v2.

**Other improvements I suggest:**

1. **A hook question that is answered at the end** of each episode (for example, "What's the difference between *I play football* and *I'm playing football*?"). It gives students a reason to keep watching.
2. **Three-question quick quiz** with a 3-2-1 countdown and "Say your answers out loud!", which links the series to the familiar vocab-video routine.
3. **"Pause and screenshot" cheat sheet**: one screen that condenses the textbook section into a revision card.
4. **YouTube captions file (SRT)** for every video. This supports weaker readers and listeners without cluttering the screen.
5. **YouTube title, description and chapters** with every video, in the same style as the vocab series, never mentioning AI.
6. *(Later, optional)* a 30 to 45-second vertical Short cut from each cheat sheet and quiz, for Instagram or YouTube Shorts.

## 8. What happens after approval

1. **Kayla is finalised** from your chosen design: a master image, a green-screen keyframe and the avatar stills. You see them before anything else is built.
2. **Library lines are recorded** in the chosen voice and checked for "K S Lo", pronunciation and falling endings. The three library clips are then generated, two variants each, and checked for lip-sync and gestures that match the words.
3. **Mockup stills** of the new layout are shown to you for approval: the opening lockup, a teaching screen with Kayla's facecam, a timeline, a quiz screen, the cheat sheet and the end card.
4. **Episode 1 is built in full**: narration, rendering, a speech check against the script and a visual check of every scene. It is delivered with its YouTube text and captions file. Episodes 2 to 4 then follow the same pipeline at much lower cost.
5. A **reusable skill for the grammar series** is written as a complete record and failsafe, in the same way as the vocab-video skill.
