## How Kayla appears during the explanations

Kayla is a **cut-out with no box or frame**, like a VTuber model overlaid on a live stream. For most of the video she stands in the **bottom-right corner**, shown from the waist up. The bottom edge of the screen cuts her off, and she fills about two-thirds of the screen height. All teaching content sits in a panel on the left that she never covers. She is animated throughout: her mouth moves with her voice, and she blinks and sways gently. She also changes expression on cue: a wink for correct answers, a surprised face for "Plot twist!" and a thinking face during quiz countdowns. At **pop-up moments** (the hook, level-ups and quiz answers) she slides in larger. A small "facecam" window would make her too small to feel like a character, so I have not used one.

## Core idea: four levels on one Tense Map

The video is organised like a game with four levels. A **Tense Map** ties the levels together. It is a 2 x 2 table that turns the textbook's lists into one picture students can remember:

| | **Simple** (yellow, shown as dots: whole, fact, habit, finished) | **Continuous** (pink, shown as bars: in progress) |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | simple present | present continuous |
| **Past** | simple past | past continuous |

Each level lights up part of the map, and a progress bar in the header shows how far students have got. **Level 3** covers verbs that never go pink: the "No -ing Zone". **Level 4** shows stories switching between the past row and the present row. The finished map is the **"pause and screenshot" cheat sheet** at the end.

**The hook is the textbook's own opening page.** Kayla shows the two Snow White speech bubbles from page 52, in which *is*, *read*, *was*, *reaches*, *gives*, *saves* and *live* mix the present and the past, and asks, "Is that allowed?" The video answers the question in Level 4, so there is a reason to keep watching to the end.

**Examples left out to save time:** *We often walk our dog in the morning.*, *My grandpa passed away two years ago.* (also a sensitive example for a cheerful host), *The cake smelt great.* and the Snow White synopsis bubble as a separate scene (it now forms the hook instead). **Why this works as one video:** students see the four tenses as one system rather than four separate lists. The level structure gives regular "checkpoints" that reset attention about every minute. YouTube chapters (Intro, Level 1 to Level 4, Boss quiz, Cheat sheet) let students jump straight to the part they need for revision.
